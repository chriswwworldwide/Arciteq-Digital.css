Puppy toilet training timeline — what's normal at 8, 12, 16 and 20 weeks

By the PupStartHQ team · Updated 4 October 2026

Most puppies are reliably clean by four to six months. 'Reliably' is the key word — a dry fortnight at ten weeks is routine, not graduation. This timeline tells you what's normal at each stage so you stop worrying at the wrong moments.

8–10 weeks: you do the work

Bladder control is minimal. Out every 45–60 minutes while awake, after sleep, meals and play. Reward outside, within two seconds of finishing. Indoors, supervise or crate — a puppy loose in another room is an accident in progress.

10–12 weeks: the first dry days

Gaps stretch to 60–90 minutes. You'll see the first signs — circling, sniffing, heading for the door. Accidents now are almost always a missed cue or an over-long gap, not the puppy's fault.

12–16 weeks: mostly clean, still supervised

Two-hour gaps, sleeping through most nights. This is where owners relax too soon: freedom of the house comes in stages, one room at a time, as each stays clean for two weeks.

16–24 weeks: reliability

Three to four hours between trips. Occasional accidents around teething, excitement or a change in routine are normal. If accidents suddenly increase, see the vet — urinary infections are common in puppies.

Washable pads vs disposable

Disposable pads get shredded and teach the puppy that paper is a toilet. A washable pad with a waterproof back by the door is a safe backup for when you can't get out in time, costs less over a year and doesn't fill the bin. Use it as a fallback, not the goal.

Cleaning accidents so they don't repeat

Blot, then use an enzyme cleaner — not bleach or ammonia-based products, which smell like urine to a dog and invite a repeat. Never scold after the fact; the puppy cannot connect it.

Frequently asked questions

How long does puppy toilet training take?

Most puppies are mostly clean by 4–6 months and fully reliable by 6–8 months. Small breeds often take longer.

Should I use puppy pads at all?

Use a washable pad as a backup by the door for emergencies, but take the puppy outside as the main routine. Relying on pads indoors delays outdoor training.

My 5-month-old puppy has started weeing indoors again — why?

Common causes are teething, a routine change, too much freedom too soon, or a urinary infection. Tighten supervision and ask the vet if it continues more than a few days.

Is it normal for a puppy to wee from excitement?

Yes, up to about 6 months. Greet calmly, outdoors if possible, and it usually resolves on its own.
