Run Zone

Half the race is running. Most people don't train it.

Eight kilometres on legs that have just pushed a sled. Here's how to pace it, how to train it, and how to do it from a city that's 32°C at 6 a.m.

Part 01

8 × 1 km pacing

Your Hyrox run pace is not your 5 km pace. Start with your current fresh 5 km race pace and add 20–30 seconds per kilometre — that is the pace to hold on run one, and on run eight. Even splits win Hyrox; a fast opening kilometre is borrowed time you repay with interest on the sleds.

  • 5 km in 25:00Hyrox runs at ~5:20–5:30 /km
  • 5 km in 30:00Hyrox runs at ~6:20–6:30 /km

The two runs that decide your result are run three (straight off the sled pull) and run eight (into wall balls). Practise both: know what "steady" feels like when your legs are full of lactate, and rehearse the finish so you arrive at the wall with a plan, not a red face.

Dina's cue

"First kilometre is a warm-up you're being timed on." If it feels easy, it's right.

Part 02

Compromised running

Compromised running is running at a high heart rate with fatigued legs — every run in Hyrox after the first. It doesn't come from easy long runs alone. It comes from interval sessions that copy the race shape: run, station, run.

The three sessions that build it

  • Run–station–run intervals: 4–6 rounds of 800 m run → 2 minutes of sled, lunges or wall balls → 800 m run at race pace. Once a week.
  • Tempo run: 20–30 minutes at Hyrox pace, uninterrupted, so the pace itself becomes automatic. Once a week.
  • Easy aerobic run: 40–60 minutes conversational. This is the base everything else sits on; skip it and the intervals stop working. Once or twice a week.

Dina's cue

"Find your legs in the first 100 m." Off a station, shorten your stride, lift your cadence, and let the pace come back to you instead of chasing it.

Part 03

Jakarta heat training

Jakarta is hot and humid all year, and Hyrox races are indoors and air-conditioned. That mismatch matters: outdoor paces here will be 15–30 seconds per kilometre slower than your race-day pace, and that's normal, not weakness.

  • Run early or late. Before 6:30 a.m. or after 7 p.m. Midday sessions are for the treadmill.
  • Race-pace work indoors. Treadmill intervals at 1% incline for anything where the number matters; outdoors for the easy aerobic volume.
  • Electrolytes, not just water. An hour of running here loses far more salt than a European gym session. Salt tablets or an electrolyte mix in every long run.
  • Use the heat as a tool. Heat-adapted athletes hold higher blood plasma volume — your race-day aerobic engine is bigger for having trained here.

Dina's cue

"Judge outdoor runs by effort, indoor runs by pace." Two different dashboards for two different jobs.

Part 04

A sample run week

For someone training four days a week with a race 12 weeks out. Station and strength work sits on the same days — this is just the running layer.

  • TuesdayRun–station–run intervals, 5 rounds
  • ThursdayTempo 25 min at Hyrox pace
  • SaturdayEasy 50 min, outdoors, by effort
  • SundayRace simulation every 3rd week: 4 runs + 4 stations

Volume matters less than consistency. Twelve weeks of this unmissed beats twenty weeks of on-and-off.

Run FAQ

How fast should I run the 1 km runs in Hyrox?

About 20–30 seconds per km slower than your fresh 5 km pace, held evenly across all eight. Even splits beat a fast start every time.

What is compromised running?

Running with a high heart rate and tired legs straight after a strength effort — every Hyrox run after the first. Train it with run–station–run intervals.

How do I train for Hyrox in Jakarta's heat?

Early or late outdoors for easy volume, treadmill for race-pace work, electrolytes in every long run, and expect slower outdoor paces. Races are indoors, so heat sessions build fitness rather than predicting race pace.
