Cats & dogs together

A calmer, happier mixed home

Cats and dogs can be great housemates with the right setup. PawSense helps multi-pet homes create safe spaces, reduce conflict, and encourage the calm curiosity that turns into real companionship.

Who it's for

Households sharing life with both cats and dogs — whether introducing a new pet or smoothing an established but tense relationship.

Why it matters

Most cat-dog friction comes from resources, space and pace, not personality. Give each pet retreat options, control the introductions, and monitor the flashpoints, and rivalry usually settles into tolerance — often into play.

Specialist short reads

Introducing a cat and dog the right way

Go slow: scent-swap first, then barrier meetings, then short supervised sessions. Reward calm, end on a good note, and never force contact — the cat needs an easy exit at all times.

Giving the cat safe high ground

Cats settle when they can watch from above and retreat when they want. Dog-free resting spots and vertical escape routes prevent most stand-offs.

Encouraging safe shared play

Parallel play and calm shared routines build tolerance. Keep high-value toys and food separate at first to avoid guarding, and monitor the early sessions.

What mixed homes need

Everyone needs a retreat

A cat with safe high ground and a dog with its own rest spot rarely clash.

Controlled introductions

Slow, positive meetings prevent a bad first impression sticking.

Watch the flashpoints

Food, doorways and toys cause most spats — monitoring catches them early.

Frequently asked questions

Can cats and dogs really live together happily?

Very often, yes. With safe retreats, slow introductions and monitored early sessions, most cat-dog pairs settle into tolerance and frequently into play.

How do I stop my cat and dog fighting over food?

Feed them separately, keep high-value toys apart at first, and give the cat elevated, dog-free space. Most conflict is about resources, not dislike.

How long do introductions take?

Anywhere from days to several weeks. Go at the more nervous animal's pace, reward calm, and never force contact.
