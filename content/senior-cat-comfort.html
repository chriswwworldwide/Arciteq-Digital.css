Senior Cat Comfort Guide

Older cats spend more time sleeping and less time jumping. Comfort for a senior cat comes down to a few quiet wins: a bed that supports stiff joints, warm spots they do not have to climb for, gentle grooming, and a calm routine that reduces stress.

🛌 Sleep quality drops

Thin beds and cold floors leave senior cats stiff after rest. An orthopedic, heat-retaining bed helps them settle deeply and get up more easily.

🦴 Stiff joints and less jumping

Arthritis makes high perches and litter trays hard work. Low, easy access to everything reduces the jumps that cause flares.

🪮 Grooming becomes harder

Less flexible spines mean cats miss spots. A gentle grooming glove keeps their coat clean without tugging or over-bathing.

😿 Stress from change

New routines, loud homes, or cold rooms make older cats anxious. A calm, warm, predictable space is the best comfort tool.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best bed for a senior cat?

Low-sided, orthopedic, and heat-retaining. It should be easy to step into, have enough cushion for joints, and hold warmth from the cat's own body.

How can I help my senior cat groom?

Use a soft grooming glove a few times a week. It mimics petting, so it is less stressful, and it helps remove loose fur without pulling at the skin.

Is it normal for an older cat to sleep more?

Yes. Senior cats often sleep 16–20 hours a day. The key is that the sleep is comfortable and they wake without stiffness or agitation.

What are signs of joint pain in senior cats?

Hesitation before jumping, sleeping in lower places, reduced grooming, limping, and irritability when touched. A vet can confirm if pain medication is needed.

How do I make my home more senior-cat friendly?

Lower perches, warm beds, a low-sided litter tray, a grooming routine, and quiet spaces. Remove the need to jump or stretch for the basics.
