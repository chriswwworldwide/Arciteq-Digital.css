Your puppy's first week at home — a day-by-day plan

By the PupStartHQ team · Updated 4 October 2026

The first week sets the pattern for the whole first year. You don't need to teach much — you need a predictable routine, a safe space and a lot of sleep (theirs, mostly). Here is what to actually do each day, and what to ignore for now.

Before the puppy arrives

Set up one room, not the whole house: crate with the divider pulled forward so there's only room to lie down, a washable pad by the door you'll use for toilet trips, water bowl, two chew toys and the blanket from the breeder if you have one. Puppy-proof at nose height — cables, shoes, houseplants, bin.

Day 1 — arrival and the first night

Carry the puppy straight to the toilet spot before going indoors. Keep visitors away. Feed what the breeder fed, same times. Bedtime: crate beside your bed (or you on the sofa beside the crate) so you hear them stir. Expect to go out two or three times in the night; that's normal, not failure. A heartbeat comforter helps most puppies settle faster on nights one to three.

Days 2–3 — the routine takes shape

Toilet trips: on waking, after eating, after play, and every 45–60 minutes while awake. Praise quietly the second they finish outside. Start feeding meals in the crate with the door open. Short name games — say their name, treat when they look. Nothing else.

Days 4–5 — vet check and the crate door

Book the first vet visit this week: health check, vaccination schedule, microchip registration (a legal requirement in the UK). At home, start closing the crate door for a minute at a time while they chew something, then open before they fuss. Begin wearing a soft harness around the house for five minutes a day — no lead yet.

Days 6–7 — gentle exposure begins

Still no pavement walks until vaccinations are complete, but socialisation starts now: carry them to see traffic, people, a café door, a hoover from across the room. Ten minutes, then home to sleep. Puppies need 18–20 hours of sleep a day; an overtired puppy bites more and settles less.

What to leave until week two

Lead walking, 'sit' and 'stay', visiting friends' dogs, bathing, nail clipping, and anyone's advice about dominance. Routine first, tricks later.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I leave a new puppy alone?

Not at all in the first few days. From week two, build from one minute to twenty minutes in the crate with a chew while you're in another room. By 12 weeks most puppies can manage an hour or two.

Should the puppy sleep in my bedroom?

For the first week or two, yes — beside your bed in a crate. You'll hear them stir for toilet trips and they settle faster. You can move the crate to its long-term spot gradually once they sleep through.

How often does an 8-week-old puppy need the toilet?

Roughly every 45–60 minutes while awake, plus immediately after waking, eating and playing. Overnight, expect two or three trips in the first fortnight.

When can my puppy go for walks?

Pavement walks start a week after the second vaccination — typically around 12–13 weeks. Until then carry them out to see the world.
