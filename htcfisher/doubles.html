Doubles & Relay Planner

Who takes which station? It might not be what you think.

Put in your station times and your partner's. We'll tell you who should take the sled, who takes the wall balls, how to share each station and how much time the right split saves — for doubles, mixed doubles and four-person relay. Ego picks the stations in most teams. Splits should.

By Ross — HYROX Group Coach, podium athlete, Christchurch personal trainer.

Nothing here is saved. It runs in your browser — enter the times, read the plan, screenshot it for the group chat.

Step 01

Your team

Doubles: you both run every run together and share the eight stations. Relay: four of you, each takes two stations outright.

Step 02

Station times

Each athlete's time for a full station on their own, as m:ss — from a singles race result, a sim day, or an honest training test. Doubles: leave a station blank if an athlete has never done it; we'll assume the other partner takes it. Relay: blank means they don't do that station.

Step 03

Your station plan

    The surprises

      How to race it

        Station times are a yardstick. On the day, the partner who is fresher after the run takes the first reps, and nobody argues at station six.

        Step 04

        How it works

        Doubles. For each station we compare your two solo times. The faster athlete takes the bigger share, in proportion to how much faster they are — so if you're 20% quicker on the sled you take about 60% of the metres, not all of them. Doing every rep of your best station wrecks your next run, and both of you run every run. We also compare the plan with a straight 50/50 split and with "strongest takes it all" to show the time saved.

        Relay. Every possible way of giving eight stations to four athletes, two each, is checked (there are 2,520) and the fastest total wins. Then we show you what happens if everyone simply picks their favourite — usually slower, usually by minutes.
