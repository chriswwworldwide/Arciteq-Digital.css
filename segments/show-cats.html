For show cats

Benched, groomed and calm

Cat shows reward condition and composure just as much as dog shows. PawSense helps you prep coats, keep your cat calm in a busy hall, and travel safely between shows.

Who it's for

Exhibitors showing pedigree and household-pet cats under bodies like GCCF, TICA and CFA who want their cat calm, clean and well-presented at benching and judging.

Why it matters

A stressed cat in a loud hall shows poorly and recovers slowly. Careful coat prep, a familiar resting setup and calm transport protect both condition and temperament across the season.

Specialist short reads

Coat prep by type

Longhairs, shorthairs and rex coats all need different routines. Build a gentle grooming rhythm in the weeks before, and keep bathing well ahead of the show so the coat settles.

Keeping calm at benching

A familiar-smelling resting mat, a partly covered pen and a quiet arrival routine help a cat settle in a noisy hall between judging rings.

Safe transport for cats

A secure, ventilated carrier and steady temperature matter more for cats than distance. Cover part of the carrier and avoid sudden noise and light.

Show-day rundown

Cats settle best on a calm, predictable timeline.

  1. Early
    Arrive & vet-in

    Arrive in good time for the veterinary check and find your pen.

  2. Set up
    Benching & settle

    Set up a familiar, partly covered pen and let your cat settle quietly.

  3. Judging
    To the ring

    Present a clean, calm cat to the judge as called.

  4. After
    Rest & results

    Quiet, water and rest — then note the outcome for the season.

Frequently asked questions

Is there really a cat show scene?

Yes — bodies like GCCF, TICA and CFA run pedigree and household-pet shows, including large championship and 'Supreme' events.

How do I keep a show cat calm at the hall?

Bring a familiar-smelling resting mat, partly cover the pen, and keep arrival and handling quiet and predictable.

How should I transport a show cat?

Use a secure, ventilated carrier, keep temperature steady, and cover part of the carrier to reduce visual stress.
