Your kitten's first 30 days — settling in, litter tray and the first night

By the PupStartHQ team · Updated 4 October 2026

Kittens settle faster than puppies but on their own terms: give them a small space, let them come to you, and most of the 'training' happens by itself. Here's the first month, week by week.

Before arrival: the safe room

One quiet room with the door shut: low-entry litter tray in one corner, food and water in the opposite corner (never beside the tray), a bed or box with a blanket, a scratching post and a couple of toys. Block gaps behind appliances and under beds where a frightened kitten can wedge itself.

Week 1: let them come to you

Sit on the floor and read. Don't chase or lift. Feed little and often, same food as the breeder or rescue. Show the kitten the litter tray after each meal and nap by placing them in it — most take to it within a day. Scoop twice daily; kittens refuse dirty trays.

The first night

Expect crying. Leave a warm bed, a ticking clock or heartbeat toy, a night light and the tray within sight. Don't bring them into your bed on night one unless that's the plan for life. Most kittens sleep through by night three or four.

Week 2: opening the door

Let them explore one more room at a time, door to the safe room open so they can retreat. Keep the tray in the safe room for now and add a second tray on another floor if you have one — the rule is one tray per cat plus one.

Weeks 3–4: scratching, play and routine

Put the post next to whatever they've started scratching, then move it gradually. Two ten-minute wand-toy sessions a day burn the energy that otherwise lands on your ankles at 3 a.m. Vet: vaccinations at 9 and 12 weeks, microchip, worming, book neutering for around 4 months.

Common mistakes

Tray too deep for a small kitten; tray beside food; scented litter; too much house too soon; playing with hands (teaches biting); and letting an unvaccinated, un-neutered kitten outside.

Frequently asked questions

How do I litter train a kitten?

Most kittens are already trained by their mother. Use a low-entry tray with unscented litter, place the kitten in it after meals and naps, keep it clean and don't move it. Accidents usually mean the tray is wrong, dirty or too far away.

Should the kitten sleep in my bedroom?

Only if you want that for the long term — kittens form habits in the first fortnight. Otherwise a warm, enclosed bed in the safe room with a night light.

When can my kitten go outside?

Not until fully vaccinated and neutered — typically 5–6 months — and then in short supervised sessions at first.

Why is my kitten biting my hands?

Because hands became toys. Switch to wand toys, go still and end play when teeth touch skin.
