Pet camera buying guide — what actually matters when they're home alone

By the PawVigil team · Updated 4 October 2026

Most people buy a pet camera to answer one question: is she okay? The features that answer it are not the ones on the box. Here's what's worth paying for, what isn't, and where to put the thing.

Field of view and placement

A 110–130° lens at hip height covers a living room from a corner. Wider than that distorts and makes the pet tiny on screen. Point it at where the pet actually spends the day — the sofa, the bed, the door they wait by — not the room centre.

Night vision and low light

Curtains closed, winter afternoons: half of 'home alone' is dim. Infrared night vision is standard; what varies is quality. Look for 1080p with a stated low-light mode rather than 2K marketing.

Two-way audio: use carefully

Hearing your voice with no you attached confuses some dogs and ramps up anxiety. Try it once while you're in the next room and watch the reaction. For many pets, silent watching is kinder.

Treat tossers and lasers

Fun, but they teach the pet to stare at the camera waiting for food, and a flung treat on a hard floor startles nervous dogs. Fine for a confident, food-motivated dog; skip for anxious ones.

Alerts that are useful

Bark/meow detection with a sensitivity slider and motion zones — so you're told when the dog is crying at the door, not every time the heating clicks. Continuous recording is unnecessary; event clips are enough.

Cloud vs local storage and privacy

Cloud plans run £3–10 a month; a microSD card costs £10 once. Either way the camera is a networked device in your home: change the default password, enable two-factor login, and turn on a privacy shutter or schedule when you're home.

What a camera won't do

It won't reduce separation anxiety; it shows you the extent of it. If the footage shows pacing, howling or destruction for most of the day, that's a training and routine problem — a camera plus a dog walker beats a camera alone.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a subscription for a pet camera?

Not if the camera records to a microSD card. Cloud subscriptions add remote backup and longer history but are optional on most models.

Will my dog be upset hearing my voice through the camera?

Some are. Test it from another room first; if the dog searches the house for you, keep audio one-way.

Where should I put a pet camera?

Corner of the room at around 1 metre, aimed at the spot the pet rests or waits, away from direct window glare.

Are pet cameras safe from hackers?

Reasonably, if you change the default password, enable two-factor authentication and keep firmware updated. Avoid no-name brands with no update history.
