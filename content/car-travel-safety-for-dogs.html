Car Travel Safety for Dogs

Most dogs love the car until something goes wrong: a sudden stop, a hot cabin, or a door that opens onto a busy road. Almost every car incident with a dog comes down to one of four things, and each has a simple, non-ingestible fix.

🛑 Unrestrained in a stop

A loose dog becomes a projectile in a sudden brake and a distraction before it. A crash-tested tether clipped to a harness (never a collar) keeps them seated and keeps you watching the road.

🌡️ Heat build-up

A parked car can reach dangerous temperatures within minutes, even with a cracked window. Never leave a dog in a parked car; plan stops where they come with you.

🤢 Motion sickness and stress

Puppies and anxious dogs often drool, whine or vomit. A forward-facing, low-vibration seat position, fresh air and short practice drives fix most of it without medication.

🚪 Bolting at the door

Service stations and car parks are where dogs get lost. Clip the lead on before the door opens, and make sure ID is on the dog, not the car.

The car travel kit

Restraint, identification and a way to find them if the worst happens:

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe for a dog to ride in the front seat?

No. Airbags are designed for adults and can seriously injure a dog. Dogs belong in the back seat, restrained with a tether and harness, or in a secured crate.

Can I attach a car tether to my dog's collar?

Never. In a sudden stop a collar tether can cause neck injury. Always clip the tether to a well-fitted harness.

How often should I stop on a long drive with a dog?

Every two to three hours for water, a toilet break and a short walk. Clip the lead on before you open the door.

How do I help a dog that gets car sick?

Start with short, calm drives that end somewhere fun, skip meals two hours before travel, keep the car cool with fresh air, and face them forward. Speak to your vet if it persists.

How hot is too hot to leave a dog in a car?

There is no safe temperature or duration. Cabins heat far faster than the outside air, so take your dog with you or leave them at home.
