Home-Alone Anxiety in Dogs

Chewed door frames, barking complaints from neighbours, a dog that shadows you to the door: separation anxiety is one of the most common and most fixable problems in pet homes. The goal is not to stop leaving, it is to make leaving boring.

🔊 Barking and howling

Vocalising starts within minutes of you leaving and can go on for hours. A camera with two-way audio shows you when it starts and lets you reassure without coming home.

🪑 Destructive chewing

Doors, skirting and cushions take the hit. Usually a sign of stress, not spite; a safe den space and something to do beats punishment every time.

🚶 Pacing and restlessness

Some dogs never settle. An activity tracker shows you whether they rest while you are out, which tells you if the plan is working.

💧 Drooling and accidents

House-trained dogs having accidents when alone is a stress signal. Shorter absences, a calm bed and predictable routines bring it back down.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my dog has separation anxiety?

Look for barking, destruction, pacing or accidents that only happen when you are out, plus distress as you get ready to leave. A camera confirms the pattern quickly.

How long can a dog be left alone?

Adult dogs generally cope with four to six hours; puppies and seniors need far less. Build up gradually and use a camera or tracker to check they settle.

Does a pet camera help with separation anxiety?

It helps you diagnose the problem and time your training, and two-way audio can calm some dogs. It works best alongside gradual departures, not instead of them.

Should I punish my dog for chewing when I was out?

No. Dogs do not connect punishment to something they did hours ago; it only adds stress. Remove the temptation, give them a safe space and reward calm.

When should I see a vet or behaviourist?

If your dog injures themselves, cannot be left for even a few minutes, or shows no improvement after a few weeks of gradual training, get professional help.
