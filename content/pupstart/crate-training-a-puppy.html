Crate training a puppy without tears — the 14-day method

By the PupStartHQ team · Updated 4 October 2026

A crate is not a cage; done properly it becomes the place your puppy chooses to sleep. The trick is that the puppy must never learn that crying opens the door — and must never be in there long enough to need to cry. Here's the two-week version that works for most puppies.

Choosing the crate and setting the divider

Buy for the adult size and use the divider. The space should be just big enough to stand, turn and lie flat. Too much room and the puppy toilets at one end and sleeps at the other — and the whole point is lost. Move the divider back every two to three weeks as they grow.

Days 1–3: the crate is where good things happen

Door open, always. Feed every meal inside. Drop treats in when the puppy isn't looking so they find them. Put the chew toy in there and nowhere else. Never push the puppy in.

Days 4–7: the door closes — briefly

While they eat or chew, swing the door to, then open it before they finish. Build to a minute, two minutes, five — always opening before any fuss. If they cry, you've gone too fast: go back a step, don't open the door on crying but wait for a two-second pause.

Days 8–14: you leave the room

Close the door, step out of sight, return in ten seconds. Build to twenty minutes over the week. Pair every session with a long-lasting chew so the crate equals the best chew of the day.

Night-time

Crate beside your bed for the first two weeks. Last toilet trip, into the crate with a comforter, lights off, no talking. If they cry in the night, take them out to toilet silently and straight back — no play, no fuss. Most puppies sleep through by 10–12 weeks.

How long is too long

A rough rule: one hour per month of age, maximum four hours, and never overnight beyond what they can physically hold. The crate is a bedroom, not day care.

Frequently asked questions

My puppy cries in the crate — what do I do?

Check they don't need the toilet. If not, wait for a two-second pause in the crying before you respond, so you reward quiet, not noise. Then shorten the next session — you've probably built up too fast.

Should I cover the crate?

Many puppies settle faster with a blanket over three sides at night; leave the front open for airflow. If they pull the blanket in and chew it, remove it.

Can I put water in the crate?

A clip-on bowl is fine during the day. Remove it for the last hour before bed and overnight while toilet training.

What size crate does my puppy need?

Measure the expected adult length nose to tail base and add 10 cm; buy that size with a divider so you can shrink it for now.
