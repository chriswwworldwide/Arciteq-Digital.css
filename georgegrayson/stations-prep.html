Stations Prep

All 8 Hyrox stations, coached.

Eight stations. Eight chances to race smarter. Explore movement cues, pacing ideas and common mistakes — then check the current rules and standards for your division.

The format: 8 × 1 km run, a station after every run, same order every race. Standards below are the women's divisions as published in recent seasons — check the current rulebook on hyrox.com before race day, weights do get revised.

Station 01

SkiErg — 1000 m

  • Distance1000 m, damper your choice
  • Target4:15–5:00 for most women's Open

Your first station arrives with a fresh heart rate from run one, so the trap is attacking it. Set a pace you could hold for 1500 m, not 1000 m. The SkiErg rewards long, hinged pulls: hips back, arms finish past the hips, then let the machine bring you up.

George's cue

"Fold, don't pull." Think of a kettlebell swing with your arms along for the ride. Shoulders that burn by 400 m mean your hips stopped working.

Station 02

Sled Push — 4 × 12.5 m

  • Women Open102 kg incl. sled
  • Women Pro152 kg incl. sled

The station that decides most first races. Every metre you push with locked knees and a high chest costs you the next run. Get low, arms straight, hands on the low poles, and drive with short choppy steps. Rest at the turns if you must, never mid-lane.

George's cue

"Nose over the sled." If you can see the far wall, you're too upright. Treat the 50 m as four sprints with a breath at each turn.

Station 03

Sled Pull — 4 × 12.5 m

  • Women Open78 kg incl. sled
  • Women Pro103 kg incl. sled

Hand-over-hand is slow; the fast athletes lean back, use their body weight and walk backwards inside the box, letting the rope pile up. Keep the rope low and your feet behind the line — stepping on the rope or over the line earns a penalty.

George's cue

"Sit into it." Your legs are stronger than your arms; the rope is just a handle for a heavy backwards squat.

Station 04

Burpee Broad Jumps — 80 m

  • Distance80 m, chest to floor each rep
  • Target~35–45 reps at a steady rhythm

Pure rhythm. Big jumps feel heroic and blow your heart rate; a shorter, metronomic jump with no pause on the floor is quicker over 80 m. Land soft, step or jump the feet in, chest down, up, jump.

George's cue

"Same jump every rep." Pick a floor marking two metres ahead and hit it every time.

Station 05

Rowing — 1000 m

  • Distance1000 m, damper your choice
  • Target4:30–5:15 for most women's Open

Halfway, and the only place you sit down. Use it: a controlled row at 24–28 strokes per minute lets your legs recover from the jumps while your split stays honest. Legs, then body, then arms — most beginners row backwards.

George's cue

"Slow slide, fast drive." The recovery is where your breathing comes back; don't rush the chain forward.

Station 06

Farmers Carry — 200 m

  • Women Open2 × 16 kg kettlebells
  • Women Pro2 × 24 kg kettlebells

A grip and posture test, not a strength test. Squeeze the handles, shoulders down and back, and walk fast with small steps. Set the bells down once at most — every drop costs 10 seconds and a restart.

George's cue

"Tall and quick." If your shoulders roll forward, the bells start swinging and your grip goes.

Station 07

Sandbag Lunges — 100 m

  • Women Open10 kg sandbag on shoulders
  • Women Pro20 kg sandbag on shoulders

Back knee touches the floor every step, bag stays on the shoulders. Slow and unbroken beats fast with stops. Keep the steps moderate — an over-long stride kills your quads before wall balls.

George's cue

"Knee kisses the floor." Tap and go; hanging out at the bottom is where the burn builds.

Station 08

Wall Balls — 75 / 100 reps

  • Women Open75 reps · 4 kg · 2.7 m target
  • Women Pro100 reps · 6 kg · 2.7 m target

The finish line is behind this wall. Plan your sets before you start — 15s or 10s with a two-breath rest — and never go to failure. Full-depth squat, ball hits above the line, or it's a no-rep and the judge will make you repeat it.

George's cue

"Catch high, squat once." Catch the ball at the chest on the way down so the descent is one movement, not two.

Strength Zone

Gym lifts that make stations faster — without doing the station

You can't push a sled every day. These are the lifts George programmes because they carry over to a specific station: they build the exact muscles and positions the station punishes, so the station gets faster while your body gets a rest from it. Two or three of these a week, heavy and well-rested, beat daily station grinding.

George's list — edits follow the programming as it evolves. Ask about any of these via Ask George.

  • Bulgarian split squat → Sled Push · Sandbag Lunges · Runs 3–8 Single-leg strength at the exact knee angle you drive the sled from, plus the hip stability that stops the lunge wobble at rep 60. Heavy, slow, 3 × 8 each leg. The single most useful Hyrox lift for women.
  • Heavy sled drags backwards (or reverse band walks) → Sled Pull · Sled Push Trains the quads and the low-position hip hinge without the carpet friction beating you up. If your gym has no sled, a heavy cable row from a low squat does the pull pattern.
  • Trap-bar deadlift → Farmers Carry · Sandbag pick-ups · Sled Pull Grip, upper back and hips together. Farmers carry is really a grip-and-posture test — 3 × 5 heavy trap-bar pulls build both faster than carrying kettlebells around the gym.
  • Suitcase carry → Farmers Carry · Runs after the carry One heavy kettlebell, walk 40 m each side. Builds the side-trunk strength that keeps you upright at 24 kg per hand, and fixes the lean that makes the next run feel drunk.
  • Barbell hip thrust → Burpee Broad Jumps · Wall Balls · Runs Glute power for the jump and for standing up out of 75 squats. Heavy 3 × 8, and a lighter explosive set of 10 in race season.
  • Front squat → Wall Balls · Sandbag Lunges Upright torso under a load held at the chest — that is a wall ball and a sandbag lunge. 3 × 6 with a pause at the bottom teaches your body the depth the judge wants.
  • Deficit push-up / push press → Wall Balls · Burpee Broad Jumps Shoulders and triceps fail before legs on the wall. Push press trains the drive-through-the-ball timing; deficit push-ups make 20+ burpee chest-to-floor reps feel like nothing.
  • Straight-arm pulldown & hollow hold → SkiErg · Rowing SkiErg is a lat-and-trunk movement; rowing is a trunk-and-lat movement. Straight-arm pulldowns (3 × 12) plus 3 × 45 s hollow holds stop the "arms only" fade at 600 m.
  • Step-ups onto a high box (weighted) → Sandbag Lunges · Compromised running Knee drive and glute under load at running-like joint angles. Great substitute for lunges when you're managing knees — or training around real life, as George did.

How to use it

Pick the two stations that cost you the most time on your last result, take their lifts, and do them twice a week for six weeks. Then re-test the station. That's how a programme is built — the coached plans do exactly this for you.

Stations FAQ

What order are the Hyrox stations in?

Fixed order every race: SkiErg, sled push, sled pull, burpee broad jumps, rowing, farmers carry, sandbag lunges, wall balls — with a 1 km run before each.

How heavy is the sled for women in Hyrox?

Women's Open: 102 kg push, 78 kg pull (including the sled). Women's Pro: 152 kg push, 103 kg pull. Check the current season's rulebook before you race.

Which station costs first-timers the most time?

Sled push and wall balls. Both punish bad pacing. Practise them tired — after a run — not fresh.

Can I prepare for HYROX without a specialist gym?

You can build useful strength and conditioning with basic gym equipment. Ask George about suitable substitutions and arrange practice on race equipment where possible.
