Puppy socialisation checklist — the 3 to 14 week window

By the PupStartHQ team · Updated 4 October 2026

Between roughly three and fourteen weeks your puppy decides what is normal. Anything they meet calmly now, they'll accept for life; anything they don't meet, they may fear. The window closes before vaccinations finish, so you cannot wait for walks — you carry them out and let the world come to them.

How to do it (the rule that matters)

Every experience should be short, at a distance where the puppy is curious not worried, and followed by something nice. One good exposure beats ten overwhelming ones. If the puppy backs off, you're too close — move away and try again.

People

Men with beards, hats, hi-vis, umbrellas, walking sticks, pushchairs, children at a distance, joggers, cyclists, the postie, a delivery driver. Aim for ten different people a week, most of them just seen, a few meeting the puppy gently.

Sounds

Hoover, washing machine, hairdryer, doorbell, fireworks and thunder recordings at low volume, traffic, a train station from the car park, a pub garden. Play sounds during meals so they're background to something good.

Surfaces and places

Grass, gravel, wet pavement, a metal drain cover, wooden decking, stairs, a lift, the vet's waiting room (just to sit and leave), the groomer's doorway, a car journey of five minutes that ends somewhere fun.

Handling

Daily: touch ears, paws, mouth, tail; hold the collar; lift them; brush for ten seconds; look in the mouth. Pair each with a treat. This is what makes vet and groomer visits uneventful later.

Other dogs

Only known, vaccinated, calm adult dogs in the garden before vaccinations are complete. A well-run puppy class (small, vet-checked, reward-based) from 10–12 weeks is worth more than a dozen dog-park meetings.

Frequently asked questions

Can I socialise my puppy before vaccinations are finished?

Yes, and you must — the window closes before the course does. Carry the puppy, use the car, visit vaccinated adult dogs in private gardens. Just keep them off public ground where unknown dogs toilet.

What if my puppy is already 14 weeks?

You've missed the easiest window, not the only one. Follow the same list, go slower and expect more wariness. Reward-based classes help a great deal.

How many new things a day?

Two or three short, positive exposures a day is plenty. Puppies also need 18 hours of sleep; an overtired puppy learns fear faster than confidence.

My puppy is scared of something on the list — push through?

No. Increase distance until they're curious again, pair with treats and build up over days. Flooding a puppy with a fear makes it permanent.
